The problems and their tickets

Shared parser: it reads dates month first while the affected tenants write day first. R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July. App, email and booking-v2 form reports use this path under the exported model. The desk’s flagged date-shift reports are excluded from this ticket because their descriptions do not establish that parser mechanism.

Form hint: booking-v3 displays MM/DD/YYYY while interpreting the entry in the day-first tenant’s convention. R-41230 describes 6 January becoming 1 June. This needs its own fix: correcting the shared parser does not correct misleading form guidance. The relevant tenant, form version and channel must be considered together.

One-day / cross-city date shifts: the desk flagged reports whose descriptions differ from a month/day swap. R-41202 says Tuesday 12 May became Monday 11 May; R-41620 says Tuesday 6 October appeared as Monday. Other reports describe differing local days or an invite arriving before booking. They get a separate investigation ticket now. A proven common cause is not required to preserve and investigate this distinct reported problem. A timezone or calendar-conversion defect remains a hypothesis, not a diagnosis.

Why the old triage decision is insufficient

Decision 0001 and the triage guide merge on normalized subject alone. Their accepted status does not establish that the rule remains adequate. The incident page calls the merged reports one parser issue with one fix queued, but its own table separates parser and hint. Even that table files the flagged reports by tenant instead of reading their symptoms. Its “no ticket of its own” wording is historical export output, not the status of the linked issue records above.

The original classifier and accepted records remain unchanged so this correction is traceable. The new problems.ts allocation applies the desk notes before the classifier; its counts are ticket coverage, not a claim that every report’s root cause is proved.

Proposed triage rule

  1. Preserve each original report and count each ID once. Reconcile conflicting duplicates rather than silently choosing one record.
  2. Read the body as well as the subject. Check tenant locale, form version and channel; intake labels and browser locale alone do not establish the mechanism.
  3. Use the supplied classifier as evidence about the input path. When a report does not fit its explanation, track that discrepancy separately instead of merging it into a known fix merely because the classifier returns the same label.
  4. Open an investigation for a distinct reported problem even while its cause is unknown. Record what is observed and what evidence would confirm or reject a suspected cause. Split that investigation further if reproduction establishes different defects.
  5. Verify the parser and form-hint corrections independently. For date-shift reports, obtain original input, stored values, intended date or instant, and the relevant zones before diagnosing a conversion. Keep report links when moving or splitting work.

Receipt and consolidation are different events

A rise in merges can reflect consolidation of older reports, not the start of a failure. The chart groups source date portions into Monday–Sunday weeks; it does not convert them to UTC. The coverage table uses received dates. Neither a fresh render nor this page’s review date refreshes the underlying export.

Evidence boundaries and follow-up

The export supplies reported symptoms and a model of the two input paths. It supplies no production implementation, reproduced failures or effective-dated tenant history. Historical parser/hint assignments therefore use the exported directory. Reports without exact input/output dates remain weaker evidence, even when assigned to a ticket.

The separate investigation preserves all flagged bodies. In particular, different local calendar days in Lisbon and Toronto can represent the same instant correctly. R-41344 also reports that the meeting was held twice; that scheduling complaint needs investigation, not an assumed timezone diagnosis. The invite-before-booking claim needs its timestamps. Do not close these reports as a parser fix without evidence explaining their behavior.
